Your lawn is off duty, not indestructible. Winter lawn care around Sioux Falls is mostly about avoiding the little habits that turn into ugly spring surprises.

Does walking on frozen grass damage the lawn?

Repeated traffic can add stress to winter turf, especially when the grass is frozen or already injured. Use cleared paths where practical instead of turning the same strip of lawn into a daily shortcut. One trip across the yard is not a botanical emergency, but repeated foot or vehicle traffic deserves attention. When a thaw leaves the ground soft, keep heavy equipment off it. Saving ten steps is less exciting when it leaves a spring repair project.

Can sidewalk salt kill the grass along my driveway?

Yes. Deicing salts can injure grass and other landscape plants, especially where salty runoff or shoveled snow collects. Remove snow mechanically first, then use only the amount of deicer recommended on the label. Keep it targeted to pavement and avoid dumping salty snow onto the lawn when practical. More salt is not automatically more effective. That familiar brown strip beside the driveway may be a winter souvenir, not a mysterious spring mowing problem.

What are the matted patches on my lawn after snow melts?

Snow mold is one possibility, especially when patches look flattened and have grayish or pinkish fungal growth after prolonged snow cover. Other winter injuries can look similar, so do not diagnose the whole lawn from one ugly patch. Once the ground is firm and the grass is dry enough, gently rake affected areas to improve drying. Many patches recover; badly damaged areas may need repair. Start with patience, not a panic-shopping cart of lawn chemicals.
